About cookies

Cookies are small text files that a website may store in your browser or on your device. They can help a site remember information between pages or visits, such as a session or a preference. Similar technologies, including local storage and pixels, may serve related purposes.

In this policy, “Professional Studio,” “we,” and “our” refer to the Professional Studio website and services.

How cookies may be used

Depending on which parts of Professional Studio you use, cookies or similar technologies may support the following purposes:

  • Keeping essential site functions and security working.
  • Remembering choices you make, such as language or display preferences.
  • Understanding how visitors use pages so the experience can be maintained and improved.
  • Supporting embedded content or services provided by other companies.

We do not list specific cookie names, providers, or expiration periods here because those details can change with the website’s configuration.

Types of cookies

YOUR PREFERENCES

Content or tools from another provider may use their own technologies, subject to that provider’s policies.

When a third-party service is embedded in a page, its provider may collect information under its own privacy and cookie terms.

Your choices

Most browsers let you view, block, or delete cookies through their privacy or site settings. You can also configure a browser to notify you when a cookie is set. The available controls vary by browser and device.

Blocking or deleting cookies may affect how some site features work. For example, features that rely on a browser session or a saved preference may need to be set up again.

If the website presents a cookie preference control, you can use it to manage the choices offered there. Browser settings remain available for controlling cookies stored by websites.

Cookies and other technologies

Some pages or embedded services may use technologies such as local storage, tags, or pixels. Browser settings may control some of these technologies, while others are managed through the feature or service that provides them.

Links to external websites are governed by those websites’ own privacy practices.
